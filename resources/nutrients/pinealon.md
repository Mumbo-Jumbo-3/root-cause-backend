# Pinealon

## What it's for

Pinealon is a synthetic tripeptide (Glu-Asp-Arg) bioregulator that works by entering the cell nucleus and binding directly to DNA sequences to upregulate expression of protective proteins. Its primary effects are concentrated in the central nervous system: neuroprotection against oxidative stress and hypoxic damage, prevention of neuronal apoptosis, enhanced neuroplasticity, and normalization of circadian rhythms and sleep-wake cycles. It also upregulates tryptophan hydroxylase, increasing serotonin synthesis — a pathway that feeds directly into melatonin production. For physical performance, pinealon appears to improve oxidative metabolic efficiency, allowing the same power output at a meaningfully lower heart rate. Because its benefits operate partly through gene expression changes, effects can persist two to four or more weeks after a dosing cycle ends.

## How to get it

Pinealon has no dietary food or drink sources. It is a synthetic tripeptide with no meaningful equivalent in whole foods.

**Supplemental forms available:**

- **Lyophilized powder vials (research grade)** — The most commonly tested form in published studies. Pure peptide with no fillers or excipients; requires reconstitution. Sold strictly for laboratory and research use; not approved for human consumption by the FDA or equivalent regulators.
- **Oral capsules** — The form used in the athletic and clinical research most often cited for performance outcomes (0.1 mg oral doses). More accessible for practical use, but product quality and excipient cleanliness vary significantly across brands.
- **Subcutaneous injection** — Also studied; requires sterile preparation and carries additional risk considerations compared to oral use.

Of available routes, oral administration has the broadest base of published research and is the most accessible form for general use. Morning dosing is preferred to leverage daytime cognitive benefits without disrupting nighttime sleep architecture.

## Dosing

No RDA, RDI, or Adequate Intake has been established for pinealon — it is not a recognized essential nutrient. All dosing is derived from research protocols.

**Research-supported oral dosing for adults:**
- **0.1 mg (100 mcg) in the morning**, with an optional second 0.1 mg dose in the afternoon or early evening
- Typical cycle length: **15–20 days on**, followed by an extended off period (weeks to months)
- Some protocols reference **100–200 mcg twice daily for 2–4 weeks**

The 15-day oral cycle at 0.1 mg produced the most cited performance outcomes in athlete studies: a 24–34% increase in physical work capacity, heart rate approximately 10 beats lower at equivalent output, a 68% reduction in stress response markers, and a 36% reduction in chromosome damage markers. Because benefits persist two to four or more weeks post-cycle due to downstream gene expression changes, continuous daily use is neither necessary nor well-supported. Morning timing is preferred; avoid late-evening dosing if sleep disruption is a concern.

## Risks

Russian clinical data accumulated over several decades reports a near-absence of side effects at the doses studied. However, this body of evidence has not been independently validated by Western regulatory bodies, and no Tolerable Upper Intake Level has been established by any regulatory authority. Pinealon is not FDA-approved as a drug or dietary supplement ingredient in the conventional sense, and commercial product purity is not guaranteed across suppliers.

Signals worth noting from available data include a potential pro-oxidant effect in some reports and a reduction in CD34+ progenitor cells — a finding relevant to blood cell production that warrants caution, particularly in individuals with hematological conditions.

**Drug and nutrient interactions:** Pinealon upregulates serotonin synthesis via tryptophan hydroxylase. Combining it with SSRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic agents carries a theoretical risk of additive serotonergic load, though no specific clinical interaction data exists. Its circadian-normalizing effects also suggest caution when stacking with sedatives, sleep medications, or other CNS-modifying compounds. Given the CD34+ signal and serotonergic mechanism, consult a physician before use if you take any prescription medication affecting the CNS, serotonin system, or blood production. Independent safety replication remains a meaningful gap in the current evidence base.
