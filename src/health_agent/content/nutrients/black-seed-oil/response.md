## What it's for

Black seed oil (*Nigella sativa*) is a multi-system functional oil whose primary active compound, **thymoquinone**, drives most of its documented benefits. It activates the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway — upregulating superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase — while suppressing the NF-κB inflammatory cascade to reduce IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α. This dual antioxidant/anti-inflammatory action underlies its benefits across neurological, metabolic, cardiovascular, hepatic, and immune systems. It increases BDNF for neuroplasticity, improves insulin sensitivity via AMPK and GLUT4 upregulation, modulates GABA/glutamate balance, regulates cortisol and histamine, and inhibits NMDA receptors to reduce excitotoxicity. It also regulates genes governing immune function, mitochondrial health, and bone growth, and can displace oxidized omega-6 PUFAs from tissue — relevant for anyone consuming industrial seed oils.

## How to get it

**Source and form**

Black seed oil comes exclusively from *Nigella sativa* seeds (also sold as black cumin seed oil or kalonji oil). The **liquid cold-pressed oil** is the preferred form over isolated extracts or capsules, as whole-oil consumption preserves the full spectrum of active compounds — thymoquinone, thymol, carvacrol, nigellone, and beta-carbolines — which likely work synergistically.

**What to look for:**
- **≥3% naturally occurring thymoquinone**, verified by third-party lab testing (e.g., Eurofins, ConsumerLab)
- **Cold-pressed and unrefined** — heat processing degrades heat-sensitive volatile compounds
- **100% pure *Nigella sativa* oil** — no added canola, sunflower, or soybean oil, which dilute potency and add pro-inflammatory omega-6 load
- **Dark glass packaging** (amber or Miron violet glass) to prevent UV degradation and oxidative rancidity
- **Batch-specific COAs** (certificates of analysis) for thymoquinone content, heavy metals, and microbial purity

**How to take it:**
- Take directly by the teaspoon/tablespoon, or mix into food — avoid cooking at high heat, which degrades thymoquinone
- Ethiopian and Turkish single-origin seeds consistently show higher TQ profiles in comparative testing

## Dosing

There is no official RDI or RDA for black seed oil. Practical guidance from clinical and practitioner sources:

- **Maintenance/general use:** ½ tsp (approximately 5–20 mg thymoquinone) daily
- **Trying to conceive:** ½ tsp twice daily maximum, and stop once pregnant (see Risks)
- **Therapeutic/repletion contexts** (e.g., inflammatory recovery, neurological support): Start at 1 tsp twice daily and work up to 1 tbsp three times daily as tolerated
- **Situational use:** 1 tsp on high electromagnetic exposure days; for air travel, 1 tbsp the night before departure, 1 tbsp before the flight, and 1 tbsp upon arrival
- **After industrial seed oil or refined sugar consumption:** A dose of black seed oil (combined with magnesium and inositol for sugar exposure) is used to counteract oxidative and inflammatory effects

Start low and increase gradually. The therapeutic upper range (1 tbsp × 3/day) is used in specific recovery protocols, not as a starting point.

## Risks

No formal Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) has been established by regulatory bodies. Key cautions:

**Pregnancy:** Thymoquinone stimulates uterine contractions, so avoid black seed oil supplements during pregnancy. When trying to conceive, limit it to ½ tsp twice daily.

**Drug interactions** — the following combinations warrant practitioner oversight:
- **Anticoagulants** (e.g., warfarin): Black seed oil inhibits platelet aggregation, increasing bleeding risk
- **Antidiabetic medications** (metformin, insulin, etc.): Additive blood sugar-lowering via AMPK/GLUT4 may cause hypoglycemia
- **SSRIs and antipsychotics:** Mechanistically plausible interaction via glutathione, GABA/glutamate, and cortisol modulation
- **Antiepileptics:** NMDA inhibition and GABAergic effects may alter seizure thresholds
- **Statins:** Possible pharmacokinetic overlap; consult before combining

**Product adulteration risk:** Oils blended with canola, sunflower, or soybean oil will not only underperform therapeutically but may actively worsen inflammation. Always verify purity via COA before purchasing.

**General side effects:** High doses may cause GI discomfort in sensitive individuals. Introduce gradually.
