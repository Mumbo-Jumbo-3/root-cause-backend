## What it's for

Vitamin D functions as a steroid hormone that regulates over 5% of the human genome. It promotes calcium absorption and maintains proper calcium/phosphate levels for bone mineralization, making it essential for bone health. Beyond bones, vitamin D modulates immune responses, promotes regulatory T-cells, and enhances antimicrobial peptide production. It influences cellular growth, neuromuscular function, and glucose metabolism through gene expression. Vitamin D also regulates gut health by controlling tight junction proteins and promoting beneficial bacteria growth, supports proper thyroid function by enabling T4 to T3 conversion, and acts as an anti-inflammatory agent throughout the body.

## How to get it

The most effective source is direct UVB exposure to skin, ideally midday sun for at least one hour daily with adequate zinc and vitamin C support. Dietary sources include wild-caught fatty fish, cod liver oil, organ meats, and egg yolks from pastured hens. For supplementation, vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) is superior to D2. Liquid oil-based forms provide better absorption when taken in morning or early afternoon with fatty meals. Essential cofactors include vitamin K2 (MK-7) at 300 mcg daily to direct calcium to bones, magnesium at 600-800 mg daily for vitamin D conversion, and vitamin A from animal foods for calcium transport.

## Dosing

Optimal blood levels should be 45-70 ng/mL for maximum benefits. For maintenance, take 2,000-4,000 IU daily. For therapeutic correction of deficiency, use 5,000-10,000 IU short-term with monitoring, then adjust based on blood levels tested after one month. The upper safe limit for long-term use is approximately 4,000 IU daily. Start supplementation based on baseline 25(OH)D blood levels and individual response, adjusting dosing accordingly.

## Risks

Vitamin D toxicity is rare but possible with excessive supplementation, causing hypercalcemia, kidney stones, and tissue calcification. Monitor blood levels especially when using high doses. Drug interactions can occur with seizure medications and other prescriptions - consult healthcare providers for specific combinations. Don't take large magnesium doses simultaneously with vitamin D. Common factors causing deficiency include limited sun exposure, sunscreen overuse, low magnesium status, high caffeine consumption, low-fat diets, very high-fiber diets, and living at high latitudes. Certain genetic variants affecting vitamin D metabolism may require adjusted dosing strategies.
