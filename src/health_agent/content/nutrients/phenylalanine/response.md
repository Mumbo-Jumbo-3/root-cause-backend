## What it's for

Phenylalanine is an essential amino acid that serves as the body's upstream raw material for several critical systems. It converts to tyrosine via the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase, which in turn drives production of dopamine, epinephrine, and norepinephrine — neurotransmitters involved in mood, motivation, focus, motor control, and stress response. Tyrosine derived from phenylalanine also contributes to melanin synthesis, affecting hair and skin pigmentation. Beyond neurotransmitter support, phenylalanine feeds into CoQ10 quinone ring synthesis, making it indirectly important for mitochondrial energy production. Insufficient intake can cascade into impaired mood, low energy, and reduced pigmentation. The D-form of phenylalanine (found in DLPA supplements) adds a complementary mechanism by slowing breakdown of endorphins and mood-relevant transmitters.

## How to get it

**Food sources** — Phenylalanine is abundant in high-protein animal foods:
- Red meat (beef, steak)
- Eggs
- Dairy (raw milk, cheese)
- Poultry and fish

Plant-based sources supply phenylalanine but are vulnerable to disruption by glyphosate, which inhibits the shikimic acid pathway in plants, potentially reducing aromatic amino acid content in plant-derived foods.

**Supplement forms:**
- **L-phenylalanine** — the naturally occurring form; enters protein synthesis directly and drives tyrosine and dopamine production. Best choice for general metabolic and performance support.
- **D-phenylalanine** — synthetic mirror image; does not enter protein synthesis but supports mood by slowing endorphin breakdown.
- **DL-phenylalanine (DLPA)** — combines both forms, offering both pathways simultaneously. Useful when both neurotransmitter precursor support and endorphin preservation are desired.

Free-form powders offer the highest bioavailability and allow precise custom dosing; capsules add convenience with minimal compromise if excipients are clean.

**Absorption tip:** Take phenylalanine in the morning paired with a small amount of sodium to improve absorption.

## Dosing

The WHO/FAO combined requirement for phenylalanine and tyrosine together is approximately **25 mg/kg/day** for adults — for a 70 kg adult, that is roughly 1,750 mg/day total from all dietary sources combined. Most people consuming adequate protein from animal foods meet this baseline without supplementation.

When supplementing beyond dietary intake — for example, to support dopamine synthesis, mood, or motivation — typical supplemental doses range from **500 mg to 1,500 mg per day**, taken in the morning with sodium to enhance absorption. No official tolerable upper intake level (UL) has been established, so conservative cycling is advisable: **limit continuous use to 4 weeks**, then take a break unless directed otherwise by a clinician. Those with specific goals (hair thinning, mood support, energy) fall in the supplemental range; those simply meeting daily protein needs do not require additional supplementation. Take a **one-week break before blood work**, as phenylalanine supplementation can skew lab results.

## Risks

**Side effects:** The most commonly reported adverse events include fatigue, nausea, diarrhea, aggravated existing conditions, and anxiety. These are generally dose-dependent and reversible. Cycling use (no more than 4 weeks continuously) reduces the risk of downregulation or accumulation effects.

**Absolute contraindication — PKU:** Individuals with phenylketonuria (PKU) cannot metabolize phenylalanine and must avoid it entirely. This is the most severe known risk group.

**Upper limits:** No official UL exists, making conservative dosing and cycling especially important.

**Drug and nutrient interactions:**
- **MAO inhibitors (MAOIs):** Phenylalanine raises dopaminergic and adrenergic tone; combining it with MAOIs risks excessive neurotransmitter accumulation, potentially causing hypertensive crisis or serotonin-related effects. Avoid this combination.
- **COMT inhibitors:** Drugs affecting COMT activity slow catecholamine breakdown; phenylalanine supplementation compounds this risk.
- **H2 blockers (H2 receptor antagonists):** Should not be combined with phenylalanine supplementation.
- **Large amounts of caffeine:** Avoid concurrent high caffeine intake.

Those with compromised liver function should exercise caution, as hepatic phenylalanine handling is sensitive to dietary protein composition.
