## What it's for

BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound 157) is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide originally isolated from human gastric juice, studied primarily for its repair and anti-inflammatory properties. Its strongest and best-characterized application is gut healing — it restores intestinal barrier integrity, protects against NSAID and alcohol damage, and accelerates closure of intestinal fistulas. Beyond the GI tract, it promotes tendon, ligament, and bone repair through angiogenesis and organized collagen deposition, and supports peripheral nerve regeneration via VEGF upregulation. It also exerts neurological effects — modulating dopamine and serotonin signaling, supporting stress resilience, and aiding TBI recovery — while showing systemic organ-level benefits for the liver, heart, and brain. All meaningful evidence comes from animal studies and small uncontrolled human pilots; no large randomized controlled trials exist.

## How to get it

BPC-157 is not present in any food or drink. It is exclusively available as a synthetic peptide in supplement or research-grade form.

**Oral forms**
- **Standard capsules** — convenient but subject to stomach acid degradation, reducing bioavailability; many commercial capsules contain fillers (microcrystalline cellulose, silicon dioxide) that vary by product.
- **Enteric-coated tablets** — resist stomach acid for better absorption than plain capsules; preferred oral form.
- Best oral use case: gut-specific healing, gut-brain axis support, systemic anti-inflammatory effects.

**Injectable forms (SubQ or IM)**
- Lyophilized powder vials reconstituted with bacteriostatic water, then injected subcutaneously or intramuscularly.
- More site-specific and faster-acting for localized musculoskeletal injuries (tendons, ligaments) than oral delivery.
- Effects are systemic regardless of injection site.
- Often combined with TB-500 and GHK-Cu in experimental recovery protocols.

**Form hierarchy by potency for localized repair:** Injectable (SubQ/IM) > enteric-coated oral > standard capsule. For gut-specific indications, oral delivery remains highly effective and is the practical first choice for most users.

## Dosing

No RDA, RDI, or AI exists for BPC-157. All figures below reflect practitioner protocols and community data, not approved clinical guidelines.

**Oral dosing**
- Standard: **500 mcg once daily**
- Maintenance or active repletion: **500 mcg twice daily (1,000 mcg total)**
- Sensitive individuals: **750 mcg/day**; higher-demand situations: up to **1,500 mcg/day**
- Best taken in the morning or pre-workout.

**Injectable dosing**
- Typical per-injection dose: **250–500 mcg**
- Active repletion or injury recovery: **0.5–1 mg every 12 hours**
- Orthopedic injury starting dose: **1 mg**, potentially higher immediately post-injury

**Cycle length**
For basic applications, 2–4 weeks is common. For injury recovery, protocols extend longer — but no continuous use should exceed **8 weeks** before taking at least an 8-week break (see Risks).

## Risks

**Angiogenesis and tumor risk — the primary concern.** BPC-157 promotes new blood vessel growth, which is central to its healing mechanism but could theoretically vascularize existing small or undetected tumors, accelerating their progression. This is not a theoretical edge case — trusted sources explicitly flag it as a reason to cycle strictly: **8 weeks on, minimum 8 weeks off**. Anyone with a personal or family history of cancer, or with undiagnosed growths, should seek physician clearance before use.

**Regulatory and evidence limitations.** BPC-157 is not FDA-approved for human use, and WADA prohibits it in sport. The products below are sold as research chemicals, labeled not for human consumption. Human data is limited to small, uncontrolled early-phase pilots (low certainty). Compounded injectable forms carry immune reaction risk if poorly sourced; no lethal dose has been established, but safety in humans remains unproven at scale.

**Drug and supplement interactions.** No serious interactions have been documented, but because BPC-157 modulates nitric oxide bidirectionally and affects dopamine and serotonin signaling, combining it with cardiovascular medications, antidepressants, or dopaminergic drugs without medical supervision is inadvisable. Always consult a physician before combining with any prescription medication.

**Sourcing risk.** Impure or contaminated research-grade peptides introduce unknown risks. Third-party HPLC and mass spec verification of each lot is the minimum quality bar.
