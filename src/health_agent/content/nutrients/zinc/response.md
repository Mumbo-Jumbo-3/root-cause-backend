# Zinc

## What it's for

Zinc is a critical trace mineral that serves as a cofactor for nearly 100-300 enzymes throughout the body. It supports immune system function and can reduce cold duration by up to 40%. Zinc is essential for hormone regulation, particularly testosterone production (can boost levels up to 50% when deficient) and helps reduce prolactin and estrogen levels. 

The mineral plays vital roles in gut health by supporting gastric acid secretion and strengthening intestinal tight junctions. It's crucial for growth and development during periods of rapid growth like childhood, adolescence, and pregnancy. Zinc supports DNA synthesis, protein building, wound healing, cellular growth and repair, and is required for endogenous antioxidant synthesis. It can also improve insulin sensitivity by 15-20% and supports skin, hair, and nail health.

## How to get it

**Food sources** provide the best approach, particularly animal-based options with highest bioavailability:
- Shellfish (especially oysters - the richest source)
- Red meat (beef, lamb) and organ meats
- Fish, seafood, eggs, and dairy products

Plant-based sources have lower bioavailability due to phytates but include pumpkin seeds (1 tbsp = 2.5mg), cashews (1 oz = 1.6mg), dark chocolate, legumes, and whole grains.

**Absorption factors:** Only 30-50% of zinc is typically absorbed. Phytates in grains and legumes reduce absorption, while vitamin C can enhance it. Heavy metals disrupt absorption.

When supplementing, the best forms are zinc glycinate, zinc picolinate, zinc bisglycinate, or zinc citrate. Avoid zinc oxide and zinc sulfate due to poor absorption.

## Dosing

**Recommended Daily Intake:**
- Men: 11mg/day
- Women: 8mg/day  
- Athletes/diabetics: 15-20mg/day

**Optimal serum levels:** 80-120 µg/dL

**Supplementation guidelines:**
- Typical maintenance: 15-30mg daily
- Short-term for deficiency: 20-45mg under supervision
- Split doses for better absorption

## Risks

The upper limit is 40mg/day to avoid toxicity. Major concerns include copper deficiency, as zinc competes with copper absorption - always pair zinc supplements with 1-2mg copper. Excess zinc can cause anemia, neurological issues, and paradoxically suppress immunity. High doses can also interfere with iron absorption.

## Interactions

Zinc competes with copper absorption, creating potential copper deficiency - supplementation should always include 1-2mg copper to maintain mineral balance. Zinc can interfere with iron absorption when taken together. Phytates in grains and legumes significantly reduce zinc absorption, while vitamin C can enhance absorption. Heavy metals including aluminum, lead, and cadmium disrupt zinc absorption.
