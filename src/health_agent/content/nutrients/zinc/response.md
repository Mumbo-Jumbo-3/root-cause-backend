# Zinc

## What it's for

Zinc is a trace mineral that serves as a cofactor for nearly 100 enzymes essential for vital cellular processes. Its primary roles include DNA creation, cell growth and multiplication, protein synthesis, and tissue repair. Zinc is critical for immune function and the gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT), making it particularly important for fighting infections and maintaining digestive health.

The mineral plays a crucial role in hormonal balance, helping produce testosterone precursor LH while reducing prolactin and estrogen levels. Research shows that zinc-deficient men who supplemented saw their testosterone levels nearly double over 6 months, while zinc deficiency can cause a 30% testosterone drop in just 16 weeks.

Zinc also supports antioxidant production by recycling vitamin C and producing endogenous antioxidants. It's particularly important during growth periods including childhood, adolescence, and pregnancy, and helps maintain digestive health by supporting gastric acid secretion and strengthening the intestinal lining.

## How to get it

The highest-quality zinc sources are animal-based foods, with shellfish (especially oysters) being the richest source. Other excellent sources include beef and red meat (grass-fed preferred), wild-caught fish and bivalves, liver, and thymus gland. Plant sources like hemp seeds/hearts and sea plants provide zinc but in less bioavailable forms.

If supplementation is necessary, zinc glycinate is preferred for biological support as both zinc and glycine support endogenous antioxidant production and liver function. Zinc picolinate and bisglycinate chelate forms offer superior bioavailability compared to basic forms like gluconate or oxide. These chelated forms are also gentler on the stomach and better absorbed.

## Dosing

Individual zinc needs vary significantly based on health history, stress levels, and life circumstances. For supplementation, start with lower doses of 15mg daily unless severely deficient. The typical supplemental range is 15-30mg of elemental zinc daily, though those with past alcohol/smoking history or higher stress may require more than healthy individuals.

The most critical aspect is maintaining proper zinc-copper balance, with a 1:1 ratio in serum levels being optimal for hormone production and immune function. When supplementing with zinc, ensure adequate copper intake through liver consumption to prevent zinc-copper imbalances.

## Risks

Zinc supplementation carries several important risks. Most significantly, excess zinc can deplete copper levels, leading to immune dysfunction and problems with mitochondrial energy production. This zinc-copper imbalance can paradoxically worsen the very systems zinc is meant to support.

Excess zinc supplementation can also cause acne, nausea, and digestive upset. Most commercial zinc supplements may contain toxic forms or contaminants, making quality sourcing essential.

Zinc interacts positively with vitamin C for detoxification and requires adequate magnesium, selenium, and other minerals for optimal function. It pairs well with glycine, cysteine/NAC, and protein for enhanced absorption. However, zinc can interfere with the absorption of other minerals when taken in high doses, particularly copper and iron.
