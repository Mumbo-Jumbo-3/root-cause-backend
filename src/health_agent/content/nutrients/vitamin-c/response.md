## What it's for

Vitamin C is a water-soluble nutrient your body cannot produce, making dietary intake essential. It serves as a powerful antioxidant and supports numerous vital functions including collagen synthesis for healthy skin, blood vessels, bones, and cartilage. Vitamin C enhances iron absorption, supports hormone production (including testosterone, progesterone, and pregnenolone), and helps regulate glucose metabolism and blood volume. It's concentrated 60:1 in white blood cells compared to surrounding fluids, making it crucial for immune function. Additional benefits include reduced oxidative stress and inflammation, faster recovery from medications, improved tendon healing, better sleep quality, and cortisol regulation.

## How to get it

The best dietary sources provide the most bioavailable vitamin C, listed by priority: acerola cherry juice (no pulp), blackberries, pomegranate juice (no pulp), kiwis, orange juice (no pulp), grapefruit juice (no pulp), strawberries, raspberries, and pineapple. Additional quality sources include citrus fruits, berries, melons, and organ meats like grass-fed liver or spleen. Always prioritize whole food sources over supplements. For optimal timing, consume vitamin C first thing in the morning, 1-2 hours after caffeine, with meals containing sodium for better absorption. Wash down supplements with acidic beverages, preferably the fruit juices listed above.

## Dosing

Start with 250mg daily for basic supplementation - this low dose is often sufficient. For therapeutic use, 500-1000mg daily split into multiple doses is appropriate. Up to 200mg daily provides rapid increases in blood and tissue levels with efficient kidney reabsorption. Above 200mg, kidney conservation becomes saturated, requiring higher doses for proportional benefits. Taking 5g daily provides about 4x the body retention compared to 100mg daily. Consider 2-3 month cycles with breaks to prevent potential negative effects from prolonged high-dose use.

## Risks

Vitamin C is generally very safe, but potential concerns include kidney stone formation (oxalates), especially with B6 deficiency. Avoid prolonged high-dose use beyond 2-3 months due to possible negative effects. Don't combine large doses with iron-rich foods at the same meal, supplemental B vitamins, aspirin and blood thinners, statins and birth control pills, or certain antibiotics. Don't use large doses if copper status is low. Enhanced medication effects may occur when combined with grapefruit juice. Higher priority individuals include smokers, those with sun exposure, heart disease risk, post-chemotherapy patients, and those recovering from alcohol use.
