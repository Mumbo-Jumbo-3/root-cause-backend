## What it's for

Inositol (most commonly supplemented as **myo-inositol**) is a six-carbon sugar alcohol that functions as a critical structural component of cell membranes and as a precursor for important signaling molecules, particularly inositol triphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG). These second messengers play central roles in intracellular calcium signaling, insulin action, and neurotransmitter pathways.

Inositol is especially important for **insulin signaling and metabolic health**. It enhances insulin sensitivity by supporting proper function of insulin receptors and glucose transporters. This makes it one of the best-researched nutrients for polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), where it improves ovulation rates, menstrual regularity, and hormonal balance — particularly when used in the evidence-based 40:1 ratio of myo-inositol to D-chiro-inositol.

It also supports **mental health and neurological function**. Inositol modulates serotonin and dopamine signaling and has been studied for its effects on anxiety, panic disorder, and mood. Because it is highly concentrated in the brain, it plays a role in neurotransmitter receptor sensitivity and overall neurological resilience.

Additional functions include support for fertility (egg quality and ovarian response), lipid metabolism, and cellular calcium homeostasis. Although historically classified as vitamin B8, inositol is not a true vitamin — the body can synthesize it — but dietary intake and supplementation can meaningfully influence its signaling roles.

## How to get it

**Food sources** provide meaningful amounts of inositol, especially in its more bioavailable forms:

- **Fruits** — particularly cantaloupe, oranges, and other citrus fruits (among the richest sources)
- **Beans and legumes** — excellent plant sources
- **Nuts and seeds**
- **Whole grains and bran**
- Smaller amounts from animal foods

Note that much of the inositol in grains and legumes exists as phytate (inositol hexaphosphate), which requires proper food preparation (soaking, sprouting, fermenting) or gut microbial activity for optimal conversion to bioavailable forms.

**Supplementation** is well-studied and generally uses:

- **Myo-inositol** — the primary and most abundant form in the body
- **D-chiro-inositol** — often combined with myo-inositol in a 40:1 ratio for PCOS and metabolic support
- **Inositol hexaphosphate (IP6)** — used in different contexts (antioxidant, mineral binding)

Myo-inositol is highly bioavailable as a powder or capsule and is well-tolerated. It works synergistically with other insulin-sensitizing nutrients such as magnesium, chromium, and alpha-lipoic acid.

## Dosing

There is no official RDA for inositol because the body produces it endogenously. However, supplemental doses used in research are well-established:

- **General support / mood**: 500–2,000 mg/day of myo-inositol
- **PCOS and metabolic health**: 2,000–4,000 mg/day total, often split into two doses (commonly as a 40:1 myo-inositol : D-chiro-inositol blend)
- **Fertility support**: 2,000–4,000 mg/day myo-inositol, frequently studied alongside folic acid

Powder forms allow flexible dosing and are often more cost-effective. Many protocols recommend taking it consistently for at least 3 months to observe full effects on hormonal and metabolic markers. It can be taken with or without food.

---

## Risks

Inositol has an excellent safety profile. The most common side effect is mild gastrointestinal discomfort (nausea, gas, or loose stools) at very high doses; this is usually minimized by splitting doses or taking with food.

**Upper limit**: No strict upper limit has been established due to low toxicity. Doses up to 18 g/day have been studied in psychiatric contexts with good tolerability.

**Considerations**:
- May enhance the effects of blood-sugar-lowering medications — monitor glucose if you have diabetes or are on insulin-sensitizing drugs
- Theoretical interaction with minerals (may bind calcium, iron, magnesium, zinc in the gut at very high doses)
- Generally considered safe during pregnancy, and some studies support its use for gestational diabetes and fertility
